    01

    Getting started

    What does IIPLA Patent Monetization do?

    IIPLA helps patent owners pursue three routes: direct patent sales, direct IP licensing of patent rights, and monetization partnerships focused on patent licensing. Our role is seller-side brokerage: screening the portfolio, preparing an agreed sell-side package and coordinating targeted discussions with buyers, licensees or specialist partners. We are not buying patents through this page or offering technology commercialization. Our scope, fees and authority are defined in a separate written engagement.

    Who can submit a portfolio? Do I need a product or IIPLA membership?

    Patent owners, companies, universities and authorized advisers can enquire about an individual patent or a portfolio. A university portfolio is welcome as a patent-rights opportunity, not a request to develop or commercialize a research project. A product launch is not required simply to submit; acceptance depends on the patent rights, evidence and counterparty fit. You do not need to purchase an IIPLA membership. Identify the legal owner and confirm authority before sharing materials.

    Can I start without a complete patent list? Why is phone required?

    Yes. Start with your name, email and phone number, including the country code. Portfolio details can follow in the optional second step. A direct number helps us clarify the enquiry and discuss next steps. Your contact permission is for this enquiry, not unrelated marketing, automated marketing calls or automatic disclosure of your details to a buyer.

    What should my initial patent asset list include?

    Provide public patent or application numbers, titles, the legal owner, countries and related patent families. Include status and priority dates where known. Briefly explain what the patents cover, relevant products or uses if known, and your preferred transaction route. Paste public references or select a non-confidential PDF, CSV or XLSX asset list. Do not submit know-how packages, source code, manufacturing instructions or product-development briefs. Request a conversation first if references are not public.

    What are encumbrances, and do they prevent a transaction?

    Encumbrances are existing rights, claims or commitments that may limit what can be sold or licensed. Examples include prior licences, sublicensing rights, a lender's security interest, co-ownership and exclusivity commitments. Also flag relevant disputes and obligations. They do not automatically rule out a review, but their effect and any required consents must be assessed. Start with a non-confidential summary; do not upload the underlying confidential agreements here.

    Does this service include technology licensing or commercialization?

    No. For this service, IP licensing means licensing patent rights. We do not offer know-how transfer, source-code delivery, product development, prototyping, technical implementation, manufacturing support or technology commercialization. Technology categories describe the subject matter of the patents, not an offer to deliver a technology package. Technical evidence may still be needed to evaluate the patent rights. In broader industry usage, technology licensing can include patents and know-how together; that broader service is outside this engagement. WIPO: IP and technology licensing background.

    02

    Review & buyer outreach

    What happens after my initial enquiry?

    The process starts by confirming your role, objectives and available public information. We then assess whether the portfolio fits the service and what further information is needed. If there is a basis to proceed, we discuss the proposed work, fees, representation and disclosure permissions in writing. Buyer outreach follows the agreed engagement; an enquiry alone does not appoint IIPLA or authorize marketing.

    What does initial patent screening or triage involve?

    Screening considers stated ownership, public patent status, territories, claim scope, disclosed restrictions and evidence of relevant product use where available. It identifies missing information and whether further patent analysis or buyer discussions are justified. It does not assess readiness to develop or launch a product. Initial screening is not a formal valuation, a validity or infringement opinion, or a substitute for buyer due diligence. Any specialist analysis is separately scoped.

    What goes into the sell-side patent package?

    The agreed package may include a patent asset schedule, owner-approved teaser, patent-family and territory summary, proposed transaction route, and known rights or restrictions. Supporting claim-to-product or evidence-of-use analysis can be included where appropriate, available and reviewed under a separate scope. This is a package for evaluating patent rights, not a technology-transfer package or product launch plan. The owner approves materials and disclosure permissions before outreach.

    How do you reach patent buyers, licensees or monetization partners?

    We plan targeted outreach through relevant IIPLA professional relationships, direct patent-market contacts and specialist advisers, matched to the patent portfolio and proposed transaction. The aim is discussions about acquiring or licensing patent rights, not finding someone to develop an invention into a product. Campaign scope, disclosure permissions and any outside support are agreed with the owner. The enquiry creates no public listing or automatic forwarding to buyers.

    Do the technology categories mean a buyer will make an offer?

    No. The 20 priority categories reflect a supplied buyer acquisition brief. The 12 additional areas support broader screening; they are not confirmed demand from that buyer. A category match does not establish patent strength, essentiality, commercial value or a purchase commitment. Individual assets, restrictions, price expectations and the proposed deal structure still need to be reviewed.

    How long does the process take, and what if there is no interest?

    Timing depends on the quality of the information, portfolio complexity, buyer response, due diligence and negotiations. Review milestones and update arrangements should be agreed in the engagement rather than assumed from this page. If interest is limited, we can discuss gaps, alternative structures or whether to pause the work. No sale, licensing income, valuation or completion date is guaranteed.

    03

    Your deal options

    How do the three patent monetization routes differ?

    A direct patent sale transfers ownership of the agreed patent rights to a buyer. Direct IP licensing allows a company to exercise specified rights under the patents while the owner retains ownership. A monetization partnership engages a specialist to pursue patent licensing or sublicensing under defined authority, with agreed sharing of proceeds. Payment structure is a separate decision. None of these service routes includes a technology-development or know-how-transfer engagement. USPTO: patent assignments and licences.

    Can I retain ownership and appoint an exclusive patent licensing partner?

    Yes, subject to a negotiated agreement and counterparty interest. For example, the owner may retain the patents while granting a partner defined sublicensing rights for specified companies. Agree the covered patents, companies, territories, duration, exclusivity, retained rights, revenue sharing, reporting and termination. That is a patent monetization partnership, not a commitment to transfer know-how or develop products. An enquiry alone grants no rights to IIPLA or anyone else.

    What are upfront, hybrid and pure backend arrangements?

    Upfront means an agreed payment at closing or another specified point. Hybrid combines an initial payment with additional payments tied to defined future events or proceeds. Pure backend means no upfront payment and compensation dependent on future monetization. These are payment structures, not ownership rules: the sale, licence or partnership agreement separately determines what rights transfer or remain with you.

    How are future or backend proceeds calculated?

    The transaction agreement must define which receipts count, any permitted cost deductions, who is paid first and the owner's share. It should also specify reporting, payment dates, currency and any audit rights. Do not assume that backend participation is a percentage of every dollar a partner earns. Future proceeds can be delayed or may never arise; agreeing to a backend structure does not secure a guaranteed return.

    Can I submit non-U.S. patents, pending applications or PCT filings?

    Yes, for a scope review. Identify any granted U.S. rights, pending U.S. applications and international family members. A foreign patent does not automatically become a U.S. patent, and a PCT application is not a worldwide patent grant. Any available U.S. filing route depends on the history, deadlines and examination requirements. A qualified patent practitioner must assess it; this enquiry does not preserve filing deadlines.

    WIPO patent guidance · PCT national-phase guidance

    Can patent licensing discussions include a settlement?

    Where relevant to a patent-rights transaction, commercial settlement discussions may be coordinated alongside the owner's legal counsel under an agreed scope. This is not a separate technology commercialization service or a promise of a recovery. Counsel handles legal strategy, infringement allegations, proceedings and settlement documentation. IIPLA's initial enquiry does not establish an attorney-client relationship or a litigation engagement.

    How is direct IP licensing different from a monetization partnership?

    In direct IP licensing, the patent owner grants a company specified rights under the patents in exchange for agreed consideration. In a monetization partnership, a specialist partner pursues patent licensing with other companies under defined authority; the arrangement may include sublicensing rights and an agreed share of receipts. Identify the counterparties, rights, exclusivity and payment obligations in writing. Neither route makes IIPLA the patent owner or obligates it to supply technology, know-how or product support.

    04

    Fees & exclusivity

    Who pays IIPLA?

    The patent owner or seller pays IIPLA's agreed sell-side commission under the written engagement. Depending on the agreed payment instructions, the owner may pay directly, or the commission may be deducted and remitted from transaction proceeds with authorization. A prospective buyer's involvement does not, by itself, make that buyer responsible for IIPLA's fee. The rate, payment trigger and remittance process must be clearly agreed.

    Does commission apply to royalties and other future payments?

    The engagement will define the owner's receipts from an IIPLA-originated transaction that attract commission, which may include upfront, deferred, milestone, royalty and other backend amounts actually received. The agreed duration and reporting obligations also matter. This is not a claim on all of the owner's future income, or automatically on all revenue a buyer later earns. An outright sale gives no automatic entitlement to the buyer's subsequent profits.

    Are there upfront fees or costs?

    Submitting an enquiry creates no payment obligation. Any patent assessment, claim analysis, sell-side preparation, representation fee, expenses or success commission must be disclosed in a separate written scope before that work begins. Third-party patent-analysis and legal costs should be separately identified. Product development, prototyping, know-how transfer and manufacturing support are outside this service. No universal commission rate or fee package is quoted here.

    Does submitting my details make IIPLA my exclusive broker?

    No. The enquiry does not create representation, exclusivity, a sale obligation or permission to bind you to a transaction. Any brokerage engagement must identify the covered assets, duration, services, fees and whether representation is exclusive or non-exclusive. Existing relationships and exceptions should be addressed before you sign. You retain the decision whether to accept an offer.

    Can a buyer request a first look or an exclusivity period?

    Yes, a prospective buyer may ask for time to review the portfolio before broader outreach, or for an exclusive negotiation window after showing interest. These are different stages, not automatic rights. Any restriction needs the owner's separate written approval, with clear assets, scope, duration, milestones and release conditions. Agree what happens if the buyer declines or the period expires; no standard lock-up is imposed by this form.

    Can I use my own lawyer or work with an existing broker?

    Yes. Tell us who already represents you and disclose any exclusive mandate, prior introductions or related obligations. We need to resolve authority, scope and potential conflicts before an engagement or overlapping outreach. Your chosen counsel can advise on the transaction documents. Any co-broker involvement and fee arrangements must be expressly agreed; they are not assumed from an introduction.

    05

    Diligence & closing

    What additional patent information might a counterparty request?

    Patent diligence may require ownership and assignment records, prosecution history, maintenance status, relevant licence or security documents, and supporting claim-to-product analysis where available. A counterparty may also seek clarification of disputes, obligations or required consents. These are checks on the patent rights and proposed transaction, not a request to deliver know-how, engineering files or a product-development package. Agree confidentiality and access arrangements first; do not upload privileged advice or restricted material here.

    How is confidentiality handled? Does the form create an NDA?

    The form does not create an NDA, an attorney-client relationship or a public listing. Use public, non-confidential information at the enquiry stage. Before sensitive follow-up, discuss the appropriate NDA, approved recipients, permitted uses and transfer channel. Information is not automatically sent to buyers by this page. The privacy and submission notices explain the intake; any engagement must separately address onward disclosures.

    Who decides whether to accept an offer?

    You do. IIPLA can help coordinate discussions and compare proposed structures within the agreed scope, but the owner decides whether to proceed. An indicative price, expression of interest or commercial discussion is not a guaranteed closing. Any term sheet should identify binding and non-binding provisions, and your counsel should review the definitive transaction documents and conditions.

    How does a transaction close, and when am I paid?

    The parties finalize the asset and rights schedules, agreed consideration, conditions and relevant sale, licence or partnership documents. Counsel coordinates the legal requirements. The contract must state the payment dates, recipient details and any agreed closing, escrow or deduction arrangements; IIPLA should not be assumed to hold the purchase money. Upfront and later payments follow the signed terms, not a timetable promised by this page.

    What happens after closing, or if I decide not to proceed?

    For deals with future payments, the agreements should identify who reports receipts, when the owner is paid and how any applicable IIPLA commission is reconciled. Any ongoing administration service needs a defined scope. If you stop, decline an offer or end an engagement, review its termination terms and any surviving confidentiality, reporting or commission obligations for covered introductions. A post-termination commission period must be expressly defined, not assumed.

    These answers describe the proposed service process, not an offer, legal opinion or signed engagement. Your written agreements govern scope, fees, rights and obligations. This public preview does not transmit or store enquiries.
